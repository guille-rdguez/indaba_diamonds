Tiny in scale ≠ Simple work

Although microdiamonds may appear simple because of their size, selecting and handling them requires exceptional concentration, experience, and precision. Every stone must be evaluated before it can become part of a finished piece of jewelry.

02

What are microdiamonds?

One stone is a detail.
Hundreds become light.

Microdiamonds, also known as melee diamonds, are very small polished diamonds commonly used to decorate engagement rings, wedding bands, earrings, pendants, watches, and other fine jewelry.

Rather than serving as the main center stone, they are normally placed around a larger diamond or arranged across a metal surface to add brilliance, texture, and visual impact.

Individually, these diamonds may be tiny. When dozens or even hundreds are placed together, they can create an impressive field of light.
03

Why are the diamonds spread across the surface?

Contrast creates
clarity.

At the beginning of the process, the specialist pours the small diamonds onto a dark, textured work surface. The contrast makes the stones easier to see and helps prevent them from rolling away.

01See

A dark ground reveals the outline and brilliance of every stone.

02Separate

Spreading the group makes individual inspection more efficient.

03Compare

Small differences become visible when stones sit side by side.

Because microdiamonds can look almost identical to the naked eye, good organization is essential. Even a small difference in diameter, color, shape, or brilliance may become noticeable once the diamonds are placed next to one another in a jewel.

The importance of matching

Many stones.
One visual rhythm.

If several small diamonds are set in a straight row, each one should have a similar diameter, color, height, and overall appearance. A stone that is slightly larger, darker, or more deeply cut can interrupt the visual flow.

Matching is especially important in pavé and micro-pavé jewelry, where many diamonds sit very close together. The surface should look continuous, balanced, and evenly illuminated. The best results appear when the individual stones blend into one sparkling composition.

06

Handling stones with precision tweezers

Pressure measured
by feel.

Microdiamonds are too small to handle safely with the fingers, so the specialist uses fine-point tweezers. The amount of pressure must be carefully controlled.

−Too little

The stone may fall.

◆Controlled

The stone stays visible and secure.

+Too much

The stone can jump away.

The diamond must also be positioned so its shape, surface, and proportions can be inspected without blocking the view. This handling may look effortless, but it requires considerable practice and excellent hand-eye coordination.

07

From sorting to setting

Four techniques.
Four expressions of light.

After the microdiamonds have been inspected and grouped, they are ready for a diamond setter. The jewelry design determines how they will be placed.

01

Pavé setting

Many small diamonds sit close together, secured with tiny beads of metal, creating a surface that appears covered with diamonds.

02

Micro-pavé setting

An even more detailed version of pavé, using extremely small diamonds and high magnification to create precise seats and equally sized metal beads.

03

Channel setting

Diamonds are placed between two parallel metal walls, creating a clean line without individual prongs between the stones.

04

Halo setting

Microdiamonds surround a larger center stone, helping it appear larger while increasing the ring’s overall brilliance.

08

Why small differences matter

Consistency is
the invisible setting.

Microdiamonds are normally viewed as a group rather than as individual gemstones. One poorly matched stone can stand out because it reflects light differently. A chipped or improperly cut stone may also appear darker once set.

Professional sorting helps ensure a uniform pattern of brilliance and allows the finished piece to meet its expected quality standards.

09

Tiny diamonds, significant craftsmanship

The stones may be small, but the attention required to inspect, sort, match, and handle them is considerable.

Each diamond is selected individually before it becomes part of the final design. This preparation allows jewelers and setters to create smooth lines, consistent sparkle, and refined detail.

Microdiamonds prove that size is not the only factor that creates beauty in jewelry. Carefully selected and precisely arranged, these tiny stones can transform a simple metal surface into an extraordinary display of brilliance.
