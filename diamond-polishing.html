Although the video mainly shows the polishing of the metal setting—apparently platinum—the process of polishing a diamond is different and requires far greater precision. Each facet must be worked individually so that light can enter the stone, reflect inside it, and return to the viewer’s eye.

02

From rough diamond to brilliant gemstone

Brilliance begins
with a plan.

A diamond freshly extracted from the earth does not have the transparent and brilliant appearance seen in a jewelry store. It usually has an irregular shape, a dull surface, and internal characteristics that must be carefully examined before cutting begins.

The process starts with detailed planning. A specialist studies the rough diamond to identify inclusions, fractures, crystal growth direction, and possible cutting shapes. The goal is to achieve the best balance between beauty, final carat weight, and value.

Beauty× Carat weight× Value
03

What is used to polish a diamond?

Only diamond
can polish diamond.

Because of its exceptional hardness, a diamond must be worked with tools coated in diamond powder. Traditionally, polishers use a rotating metal wheel known as a scaife, which is coated with a polishing compound containing microscopic diamond particles.

◆ SCAIFE · DIAMOND POWDER

The stone is held in a precision tool and carefully placed against the rotating wheel. Pressure, angle, and orientation must be constantly controlled because a diamond’s resistance to polishing can vary depending on the direction of its crystal structure.

The purpose is not simply to make the stone shine. The polisher is creating and perfecting each facet that will determine how the diamond interacts with light.

04

The main stages of diamond polishing

Five stages.
One precise result.

01

Planning and marking

Before any cutting takes place, the rough stone is analyzed to determine its final shape. It may become a round, oval, emerald, pear, cushion, or another diamond cut.

A poor decision during this stage can result in unnecessary weight loss or leave inclusions in highly visible areas.

02

Shaping the outline

The overall shape of the diamond is then created. In round diamonds, this stage helps form the girdle, the narrow edge that separates the crown from the pavilion.

The outline must be even and symmetrical so the diamond can be properly set in a piece of jewelry.

03

Creating the main facets

The specialist begins forming the principal facets of the crown and pavilion. These surfaces establish the basic proportions of the diamond and serve as guides for the remaining facets.

Important elements such as the table, pavilion, and main angles are defined during this stage.

04

Brillianteering

During brillianteering, the remaining facets are created and refined. A traditional round brilliant diamond normally has 57 or 58 facets, depending on whether it has a culet.

Each facet must have the correct position, size, and angle. Even a small deviation can affect symmetry and reduce the diamond’s brilliance.

05

Final inspection and finishing

After polishing, the diamond is inspected under magnification to detect polishing lines, surface marks, extra facets, or symmetry issues. The specialist also checks that the facet junctions are precise and that the surface has a clean finish.

When the work is performed correctly, the facets function as a combination of windows and mirrors that control the movement of light through the stone.

05

Polishing the jewelry is also important

The setting should reflect light.
Never compete with it.

The process shown in the video complements the work performed on the diamond itself. A setting with scratches, rough edges, or an uneven finish can reduce the elegance of even the finest gemstone.

By carefully bringing the ring into contact with the polishing wheel, the jeweler works on different areas until the metal has a smooth and uniform surface. When diamonds are already set in the piece, the jeweler must avoid placing too much pressure on the stones or on the small prongs that hold them in position.

The objective is to create a finish that reflects light without competing with the diamond, resulting in a clean, balanced, and professional presentation.
06

Why polishing affects brilliance

Cut controls
the journey of light.

A diamond’s beauty does not depend only on its color or clarity. The quality of its cut and polish determines how effectively it interacts with light.

01Brilliance

The white light reflected by the diamond.

02Fire

Flashes of color created when light is dispersed.

03Scintillation

The contrast between bright and dark areas when the diamond or the observer moves.

When the facets are not properly aligned, some of the light escapes through the bottom or sides of the stone. For this reason, a larger diamond is not necessarily more beautiful than a smaller diamond with a superior cut.

07

A combination of knowledge, precision, and experience

Polishing a diamond is a highly specialized craft that requires patience, coordination, and a deep understanding of the stone’s crystal structure. Every movement can affect its weight, symmetry, appearance, and value.

The video helps us appreciate that the final brilliance of a jewel does not happen automatically. It is the result of many carefully completed stages: analyzing the rough diamond, selecting the cut, forming each facet, polishing the metal setting, and inspecting the finished piece.

Behind every polished diamond is a combination of modern technology and skilled craftsmanship. This precision is what transforms a rough stone into a jewel capable of reflecting light and preserving its beauty for generations.
