When a diamond is viewed with the naked eye, the first things we usually notice are its brilliance, fire, and sparkle. Under magnification, however, the stone reveals a much more detailed world—one that may include tiny internal characteristics known as inclusions.

Although they are sometimes informally described as “impurities,” the more accurate gemological term is inclusions. These characteristics can form naturally while a diamond develops deep within the Earth under extreme heat and pressure. Because of this, each diamond can contain its own unique internal pattern—almost like a natural fingerprint.

03

How inclusions affect clarity

Clarity is not
one simple number.

Clarity is one of the 4Cs of diamond quality, alongside cut, color, and carat weight. During a professional evaluation, a gemologist studies five essential qualities of a diamond’s inclusions.

An inclusion located near the center of the diamond may be easier to notice than one positioned close to the edge. Similarly, a large or strongly contrasted inclusion can have a greater impact on the clarity grade than several microscopic features that are difficult to detect.

Are inclusions always a problem?

Not necessarily.

Many diamonds contain natural inclusions that cannot be seen without magnification. These diamonds may still appear completely clean to the naked eye and display exceptional brilliance. This is commonly described as an eye-clean diamond.

In some cases, choosing an eye-clean diamond with minor microscopic inclusions can offer an excellent balance between beauty and value. A flawless clarity grade may be rare and desirable, but it is not always necessary to achieve a visually stunning stone.

The most important factors are how noticeable the inclusions are, where they are located, and whether they interfere with the diamond’s transparency, durability, or ability to reflect light.

06

What makes every diamond unique

A natural
fingerprint.

Under magnification, a diamond becomes more than a polished gemstone. Its internal characteristics reveal part of its natural formation and help distinguish it from every other stone.

These details can also assist gemologists in identifying and documenting a specific diamond. Just as no two natural fingerprints are exactly alike, the arrangement of inclusions inside a diamond can create a unique map that belongs only to that stone.

The video demonstrates why professional inspection is such an important part of evaluating a diamond. Beauty begins with what we can see, but understanding the stone requires looking deeper.

A diamond’s inclusions are not simply imperfections—they are natural evidence of its origin, individuality, and journey from deep within the Earth to a finished piece of jewelry.
